# Expert Feedback Record — September 2026

## Status

**FACTUAL RECORD / NOT AN ENDORSEMENT / REVISION INPUT**

This note records the first substantive external feedback on *The Empty-Slot Hypothesis of Zero Cognition* v0.3.

## What happened

An inquiry and the English v0.3 research note were sent to Attila Krajcsi on 2026-06-20. A reply was received roughly three months later.

The reply did not assess novelty, validity, feasibility, or expected effect size. The expert reported that he could not reach that stage of evaluation because several core elements were not understandable at the beginning of the document.

## Where comprehension failed

### 1. The abstract was not self-contained

The first sentence referred to the “empty-slot device” before the device had been explained sufficiently for a new reader. The expert therefore skipped the abstract.

**Revision implication:** Define the experimental object in ordinary language before discussing whether it is novel or which factors are embedded in it.

### 2. The proposed stages of zero lacked a visible source and status

The proposal introduced stages of zero, but a reader could not immediately determine:

- what each stage meant,
- whether the stages came from prior literature,
- whether they were adaptations of an existing developmental model, or
- whether they were newly proposed working distinctions.

**Revision implication:** State the provenance and status of every staged model, and cite the underlying literature at the point of introduction.

### 3. “Spatial fixedness” was not intelligible as introduced

The proposal used the phrase “re-identifying the same empty state in the same place,” but this did not make the construct clear to the expert. A document search did not provide an adequate further definition where he expected one.

The later text contains a more concrete description: a fixed slot is a designated place that remains identifiable when empty. That operational description needs to appear before the term is asked to carry theoretical weight.

**Revision implication:** Prefer an observable operational description first, then introduce a compact label if the label is still useful.

### 4. Prior ideas need explicit citations

The expert emphasized that a cognitive research plan must explain its starting point in enough detail for field experts or other cognitive scientists to follow it, and that previous ideas must be cited properly when the proposal builds upon them.

**Revision implication:** Make the chain visible:

`established finding → unresolved question → proposed factor → manipulation → predicted outcome`

## What the feedback does NOT establish

This reply does **not** establish that:

- the empty-slot hypothesis is correct,
- the factor interaction is novel,
- the experiment is feasible,
- the effect exists,
- the staged model is valid, or
- the proposal has been rejected on scientific grounds.

The factual conclusion is narrower: **the v0.3 document did not communicate its starting assumptions and key constructs clearly enough for this expert to evaluate the proposal.**

## General research-method lesson

This feedback is preserved because it has value beyond zero cognition. Before asking a specialist to evaluate a new idea:

1. Start from shared literature and standard terminology.
2. Make the gap in existing work explicit.
3. Define any nonstandard concept before using it in the research question.
4. Distinguish established theory, design inference, and untested hypothesis.
5. Cite the source of inherited ideas at the point where they enter the argument.
6. Treat “the reader could not understand it” as data about the research document, not merely as an interpersonal failure.

## Next revision target

A future v0.4 should prioritize reconstruction of the opening sections before adding new experimental detail. The first page should allow a cognitive scientist unfamiliar with the project to answer four questions immediately:

1. What is already known about preschoolers’ understanding of zero?
2. What specific transition or unresolved phenomenon is being tested?
3. What exactly are the two manipulated factors?
4. Why would those factors plausibly change the primary ordering outcome?
