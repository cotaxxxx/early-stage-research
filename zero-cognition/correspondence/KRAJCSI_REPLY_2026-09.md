## Original inquiry from Katsushi Furuta

**Subject:** A factor-dissociation hypothesis concerning preschoolers’ understanding of zero

Dear Professor Krajcsi,

My name is Katsushi Furuta. I am writing from Japan.

I have prepared a short research note concerning the development of zero cognition in preschool children. The proposal does not claim that the physical device itself is novel. Instead, it formulates a factorial experiment designed to dissociate two possible mechanisms: spatial fixedness and operational reversibility.

The central question is whether repeatedly returning objects to the same empty slot, together with bidirectional operations between zero and positive quantities, facilitates the transition from operating with an empty set to classifying zero as part of the numerical sequence.

I would be very grateful for your opinion on two questions:

1. Has the interaction between spatial fixedness and operational reversibility in the operate-to-classify-zero transition already been tested?
2. Is a brief, single-session manipulation likely to affect a novel zero-ordering task, or would a multi-session micro-intervention be more plausible?

I am not a specialist in developmental psychology, and I developed this proposal through independent inquiry and dialogue with AI. I am therefore seeking expert guidance on its novelty and experimental feasibility.

I have attached the English version of the research note (v0.3). I understand that you are very busy, so even a brief comment or a reference to relevant prior work would be greatly appreciated.

Sincerely,  
Katsushi Furuta
